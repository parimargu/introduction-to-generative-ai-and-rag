Our Solar System: Quick Facts for Students

The Sun is a medium-sized star located at the centre of our solar system. It contains about 99.8 percent of all the mass in the solar system. Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth. The temperature at the surface of the Sun is about 5,500 degrees Celsius.
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest planet to the Sun. A year on Mercury lasts only 88 Earth days. Mercury has no moons and almost no atmosphere. Venus is the hottest planet, with surface temperatures around 465 degrees Celsius. Venus is hotter than Mercury because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat. Venus spins backwards compared to most planets, so the Sun rises in the west there.
Earth is the only planet known to support life. About 71 percent of Earth's surface is covered by water. Earth has one natural satellite, called the Moon. The Moon is slowly moving away from Earth at about 3.8 centimetres per year. Mars is called the Red Planet because of iron oxide, or rust, on its surface. Mars has two small moons named Phobos and Deimos. Olympus Mons on Mars is the tallest volcano in the solar system, about 22 kilometres high.
Jupiter is the largest planet in our solar system. More than 1,300 Earths could fit inside Jupiter. The Great Red Spot on Jupiter is a giant storm that has lasted for over 350 years. Jupiter has 95 officially recognised moons, and Ganymede is the biggest moon in the solar system. Saturn is famous for its bright rings made of ice and rock. Saturn is so light that it would float in a bathtub large enough to hold it. Titan, Saturn's largest moon, has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane.
Uranus rotates on its side, with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees. Uranus was the first planet discovered with a telescope, by William Herschel in 1781. Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun. Neptune has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching about 2,100 kilometres per hour. One year on Neptune lasts about 165 Earth years.
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 by the International Astronomical Union. The asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter. Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and is also a dwarf planet. Comets are icy bodies that grow glowing tails when they come close to the Sun. Halley's Comet is visible from Earth about every 76 years and will next appear in 2061.
India's Chandrayaan-3 mission landed near the Moon's south pole on 23 August 2023. India became the first country to land near the lunar south pole. The Mars Orbiter Mission, also called Mangalyaan, reached Mars orbit in September 2014. India was the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit and did it on its first attempt. Aditya-L1 is India's first space mission to study the Sun.
